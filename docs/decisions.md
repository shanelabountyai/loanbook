# LoanBook — Resolved Decisions & Open Items

The PRD leaves several rules open. The frozen fixtures in `fixtures/` encode the answers below.
If you change a decision, change the fixtures deliberately (see `fixtures/README.md`), not the tests.

## Resolved (encoded in fixtures)

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|----------|-----|
| D1 | All money is integer cents. Rates are integer basis points (1200 = 12.00%). No floats anywhere in money paths. | PRD P0-2 |
| D2 | One rounding rule: **round half up to whole cents**, implemented in exactly one module. Every rounded value is rounded once from an exact quantity. | PRD P0-2 |
| D3 | **Schedule basis:** monthly rate = APR/12; installment = half-up of the standard annuity formula; each row's interest = half-up(balance × APR/12); the **final row** pays off the remaining balance (absorbs drift). Due date k = disbursement date + k months, same day-of-month, clamped to month end (Jan 31 → Feb 28 → Mar 31). | Standard; AM1–AM5 |
| D4 | **Accrual basis:** actual/365 simple interest on outstanding principal only (no compounding). This intentionally differs from the schedule's APR/12 interest (L1 period 1: schedule 1000¢ vs accrued 1019¢). **The schedule is the plan; the ledger is the truth.** Payments allocate against *accrued* interest. | PRD P0-3 vs P0-2; AC4, WF1 |
| D5 | **Accrual rounding:** cumulative, not per-day. Within a principal segment, day *d*'s entry = round(exact interest for *d* days) − round(exact for *d−1* days). A 30-day month = 986¢, not 30 × 33¢ = 990¢. A principal change starts a new segment that rounds independently. | AC1, AC3 |
| D6 | **Accrual timing:** the run for date D posts day D's interest (balance as of start of D). Disbursement day accrues nothing. A payment on D applies after D's accrual; reduced principal takes effect D+1. A run posts every missing day through D (catch-up); re-running a date posts nothing. | AC3, AC5 |
| D7 | **Waterfall:** buckets = fees, interest, principal. Order is config (`["fees","interest","principal"]` default). Each bucket takes min(remaining payment, owed). A payment above total payoff (fees + interest + principal) is **rejected** — no credit balances. Zero/negative payments are rejected. Overpayment needs no special path: it simply reaches principal. | WF1–WF10, WFB* |
| D8 | **Chart of accounts:** the PRD's list plus `fees_receivable` (fee assessed: Dr fees_receivable / Cr fees_income) and `lender_capital` (equity, so cash has a source). Receivable and write-off accounts require a `loan_id` on the entry. | ledger fixtures |
| D9 | **Final installment amount due = payoff quote as of its due date**, not the schedule's last row, so the loan closes to exactly zero despite D4. | consequence of D4 |
| D10 | **Stack:** TypeScript on a clone of saas-foundation v1.2.0 (Next.js 16, Prisma 7, Postgres 17, vitest, Playwright), port 4700. Reference projects for conventions are storage business and rental business: money is `Int` cents with a `Cents` suffix, rates are basis points with a `Bps` suffix. Commands are npm scripts, not a Makefile. DB enforcement (balance check, append-only) is hand-written SQL inside Prisma migrations, as the foundation's `audit_append_only` migration does. Python stays only in `tools/` (the fixture oracle, stdlib only). | Decided 2026-09-29: match the house stack and reuse the foundation's auth, roles, audit and CI rather than rebuild them for Phase 4. Supersedes the Python 3.12 + pytest proposal. |
| D11 | **Integer money in TypeScript:** cents and bps are `number`, checked with `Number.isSafeInteger` at every boundary (`assertCents`, `assertBps` in `src/ledger/money.ts`). Anything else is rejected with `non_integer_amount` / `non_integer_rate`: floats, booleans, strings, bigint, NaN, values past the safe range. Exact intermediate quantities (annuity formula, actual/365) are `bigint` numerator and denominator, rounded once by `roundHalfUp` in that same module, which takes non-negative quantities only. `/`, `Math.round` and friends are lint errors in `src/ledger/**` outside `money.ts`. Known ceiling: JSON `100.0` parses to the integer `100` and is accepted, because JavaScript cannot tell them apart. | Decided 2026-09-29: matches Prisma `Int` and the reference projects, and needs no conversion at the DB or JSON edge. Rejected: `bigint` everywhere (a conversion at every Prisma and JSON boundary) and branded types (every sum re-wrapped). |
| D12 | **Ledger line amounts are `numeric` in the database, refused unless whole.** `LedgerLine.debitCents` and `creditCents` are `DECIMAL(65,30)` (Prisma `Decimal`) under a CHECK named `non_integer_amount`: a whole number, inside the safe integer range of D11. This is the one exception to D10's `Int` cents; every other money column stays `Int`. Every DB rejection carries its fixture code in the error's `constraint` field: the constraint name for a CHECK, `RAISE ... USING CONSTRAINT` for a trigger. Reads convert back to `number` through `assertCents`, at one edge. Known ceiling: `100.0` is whole and is accepted, as in D11. | Decided 2026-09-29. Measured on Postgres 17: an `integer` column stores the literal `10.5` as `11` and `10.5::float8` as `10`, and refuses a float only when it arrives as a bound parameter (22P02). The cast runs before any CHECK or trigger, so nothing on an integer column can see the original value. Rejected: `Int` with the rounding recorded as a ceiling (a ledger that cannot be corrected in place must not round silently, and those are two rounding rules, neither of them D2's), and `Int` behind a SQL write function (a second write path, ahead of task 1.6). |
| D13 | **Reversals.** `reverseEntry(entryId, date, memo)` is the only way to post one: it copies the original's loan id and lines with each line's sides swapped, and sets `LedgerEntry.reversesEntryId`. An entry is reversed at most once (unique index `already_reversed`), and only an entry that exists (foreign key `dangling_reversal`; the service raises the same code for an unknown id). A reversal may itself be reversed, which reinstates the original; nothing refuses it. The database does not check that a reversal mirrors its original; the service builds it that way, and the write-path lint rule keeps other code from posting. | Decided 2026-09-30 (task 1.8). The fixture fixes swap, reference, net zero and `already_reversed`; it names no code for an unknown original, so the verify code is reused. Reversing a reversal is ordinary bookkeeping for undoing a mistaken correction. |
| D14 | **A schedule must reach its last row with principal left to pay.** `buildSchedule` refuses (`degenerate_schedule`) terms where any row before the last would pay zero or negative principal, or would clear the balance: a tiny principal over a long term at 0% (7¢ over 12 months: installment 1¢, balance gone at row 7) would otherwise run negative. Inputs are also refused unless principal > 0 (`invalid_principal`), APR ≥ 0 (`invalid_rate`) and term a positive integer (`invalid_term`). No fixture exercises the guard; the PRD sets no principal floor. | Decided 2026-09-30, task 2.2. Rejected: capping the installment at the balance (a schedule with trailing zero rows is not a loan anyone would write). |
| D15 | **Accrual is idempotent per (loan, day) in the database.** `LedgerEntry.kind` is `'accrual'` on an accrual entry and null otherwise (CHECK `invalid_kind`); a partial unique index `already_accrued` on (`loanId`, `date`) where `kind = 'accrual'` allows one per day. `postEntry` takes the optional `kind`. `runAccrual(db, { asOf })` keeps nothing beside the ledger: principal for day D is loans_receivable dated before D, the segment starts on the date of the last such line, and the entry is cumulative(day) − accrual already posted in the segment. A day that rounds to 0 posts nothing and is carried by the next day's difference. A run that hits `already_accrued` stops on that loan: the winner is posting it, and the next run recomputes from the ledger. Known ceilings: a reversed accrual day is not re-posted (its original still holds the index); a principal line back-dated behind days already accrued is not re-segmented. Neither happens in Phase 2. | Decided 2026-09-30, task 2.5. Rejected: a separate accrual-state table (a second record of what the ledger already says), and an application-level check (two racing runs would both pass it). |
| D16 | **A payment is read, allocated and posted in one transaction under a row lock on its `Loan`.** `postPayment` does `BEGIN; SELECT … FROM "Loan" … FOR UPDATE` (the append-only trigger fires on UPDATE, not on a lock), then reads owed, allocates and posts through `postEntryInTx`, the variant of `postEntry` that leaves the transaction to the caller; `postEntry` itself is unchanged. A second payment on the loan waits for the lock, then reads the first's lines, so two payments cannot both be allowed against the same owed amount (the loser gets `exceeds_payoff`). Payments on different loans do not block each other. **`accrual_pending`:** a payment dated D is refused unless every accrual day through D that would post a non-zero amount is already posted (checked by `accrualDue`, the accrual run in dry mode), so it never allocates against stale interest (D6). Known ceiling: a payment back-dated behind accrual days already posted is not re-segmented (same as D15). | Decided 2026-10-01, task 3.3. Rejected: giving `postEntry` an optional caller transaction (every caller could then pass one by accident), and an advisory lock (a lock on the row it protects needs no key scheme). |
| D17 | **Payoff quote.** `payoffQuote(db, { loanId, asOf })` = fees + principal + interest, where interest is `interest_receivable` plus the accrual days through `asOf` the run would still post (`accrualDue`, same cumulative rule as D5/D15), **inclusive of `asOf`**: a quote for D includes D's accrual, so a payment on D at the quote closes the loan (D6 applies the payment after D's accrual). Read-only, one repeatable-read snapshot, posts and stores nothing; the result carries its inputs (`loanId`, `asOf`) and the posted/pending split, so it can be logged as is. Unknown loan: `unknown_loan`. Fixtures PO1–PO4 (`fixtures/payoff/`). Known ceiling: a quote for a date earlier than accruals already posted is not a historical balance (it still counts every posted day); quotes are for today or later. | Decided 2026-10-01, task 3.4. |
| D18 | **Delinquency bands.** Days past due (DPD) → state: 0 `current`; 1–10 `grace`; 11–59 `delinquent_30`; 60–89 `delinquent_60`; 90–119 `delinquent_90`; 120+ `default_recommended`. Closes the PRD's day 11–29 gap by making each name the floor of its bucket. Boundaries live in one config constant (`src/ledger/config.ts`), never as literals in the status code. | Decided 2026-10-01, Phase 4 start. |
| D19 | **Short payments and DPD.** An installment is satisfied when cumulative payments (cash received for the loan) ≥ cumulative scheduled installments due. DPD on date D = days since the due date of the oldest unsatisfied installment (0 if none due and unsatisfied); a due date counts as past due from the day after it. A catch-up that covers the oldest installment recomputes DPD in one step. Payments are read from the ledger (Dr cash lines for the loan), the schedule from the loan's terms, the date is the injected `asOf`; nothing is stored. Late fee 1500¢ (open item 2) confirmed by default, assessed once per missed installment at grace exit (DPD reaches 11), posted Dr fees_receivable / Cr fees_income. | Decided 2026-10-01, Phase 4 start. |
| D20 | **Late fee is idempotent per (loan, installment) in the database.** `LedgerEntry.kind` also allows `'late_fee'` (`invalid_kind`), and a new nullable `installment` column is set exactly on those entries (CHECK `invalid_installment`); a partial unique index `already_fee_assessed` on (`loanId`, `installment`) where `kind = 'late_fee'` allows one per installment. `runStatus(db, { asOf })` posts Dr fees_receivable / Cr fees_income of `LATE_FEE_CENTS`, dated the grace-exit day (due date + `GRACE_EXIT_DPD`, the floor of the first band after grace, derived in config), for each installment whose cumulative scheduled amount exceeds cash received through that day. A run behind by days posts each missed fee at its own exit date; a run that hits `already_fee_assessed` skips that installment (the winner posted it). `npm run status -- --as-of`. Known ceiling: a payment back-dated before an exit day already assessed does not reverse the fee (a waiver is P1, 4.7); reversed payments are not netted (as 4.2). | Decided 2026-10-01, task 4.3. Rejected: keying on memo text (a typo dodges the index) and a separate fee table (a second record of what the ledger says). |
| D21 | **Who sees a loan.** A loan belongs to one servicing org and one borrower through an append-only `LoanAccess(loanId PK, orgId, borrowerUserId)` row (`LoanAccess_append_only`). On the template's roles: an org's owner and admins are servicers (book overview, loan detail, statement for every loan with a row in their org); a member is a borrower and sees only the statement of a loan whose row names them. Anything else is `notFound`, the template's "not yours" rule; the guard (`requireLoan` in `src/app/o/[org]/loans/access.ts`) runs after `requireOrg`, so membership is always required too. No foreign keys: `Org`/`User` live in template-owned `core.prisma`, and `LedgerEntry.loanId` has none either (2.3); a row naming a deleted loan, org or user grants nothing. Views compute state for `?asOf=` (default today, UTC, read at the app edge, never in `src/ledger`); balances are the ledger as posted (`owedCents`/`trialBalance`). Known ceiling: `bookOverview` runs one `loanStatus` per loan. | Decided 2026-10-02, task 4.4. Rejected: `orgId` on `Loan` with a per-org `trialBalance` (every caller and the balance SQL change) and one global book (any org's admin would see every loan). |
| D22 | **Capstone and statement text.** Fixture CP1 (`fixtures/capstone/`) runs L1 day by day in the order the jobs run: accrual for D, then the late-fee job, then any payment dated D; the payoff is the D17 quote on its day. Six payments: 17255 on 02-15; 10000 on 03-15 (short); 7255 on 03-20 (inside grace, no fee); installment 3 missed, fee 04-26, 17255 on 05-01; installment 4 missed, fee 05-26, 34510 on 06-20 covering installments 4 and 5; payoff 20507 on 07-01, before installment 6 is due. `statementText(lines, asOf)` in `views.ts` narrates from ledger lines alone, entries through `asOf` in posting order: `Disbursed`, consecutive accrual entries folded into one `<from> to <to> Interest accrued` line, `Late fee`, `Payment <cash>: fees, interest, principal` (the entry's credits), anything else by its memo; then `<asOf> Owed <total>: fees, interest, principal` summed from the receivable lines. Amounts by `formatCents`. Known ceiling: a reversed entry is narrated by its shape (a reversed disbursement reads as a payment), not netted. | Decided 2026-10-02, task 4.5. The fixture holds the exact text so the format is a contract, re-derived independently in `verify_fixtures.py`. |
| D23 | **A repaid loan is closed: DPD 0, no late fee.** D19 compares cash with the schedule, but a loan closed at the quote (D17 early payoff, or the D9 final payment) has paid less than the schedule sums to, so it read as past due from its next due date and took a fee at each grace exit. Closed on D = loans_receivable through D is zero (principal is the last bucket of the waterfall, so fees and interest are zero too). `loanStatus` returns DPD 0 for a closed loan; `runStatus` skips an installment whose grace-exit day falls on or after the day principal reached zero. Fixture BK1 (`fixtures/book/`): 40 loans over 2026, ten each on_time, chronic_late, payoff_early, default, run day by day; states stored as transitions. | Decided 2026-10-02, task 4.6. Found by the simulated book: without it on-time loan 1 is delinquent after maturity. |
| D24 | **A fee waiver is the D13 reversal of a `late_fee` entry, with a required reason.** `waiveFee(db, { entryId, date, reason })` posts Dr fees_income / Cr fees_receivable for the whole fee, `reversesEntryId` = the fee, memo `Fee waived <amount> (installment <n>): <reason>` (the statement narrates it by memo, D22). Refusals, in this order: `reason_required` (blank after trimming), `dangling_reversal` (unknown entry), `not_a_fee` (not `kind = 'late_fee'`), `before_fee` (dated before the fee), then under the D16 row lock on the `Loan`: `already_reversed`, and **`fee_paid`**: fees are one pooled bucket per loan (the waterfall pays fees_receivable, not a named fee), so a waiver is allowed only if the loan's fees_receivable, running by date, stays ≥ 0 on the waiver date and every later posted date once the fee is taken off. A fee partly paid with nothing else owed is therefore refused (no partial waiver, no credit balance), and so is a back-dated waiver that would make an earlier statement owe a negative fee. **The fee keeps its installment assessed:** the original `late_fee` row stays, so `runStatus` skips it and `already_fee_assessed` refuses a second fee for that installment either way. Waivers do not touch DPD (D19 compares cash with the schedule). Fixture WV1 (`fixtures/waiver/`). Known ceiling: a payment back-dated before a waiver already posted is not re-checked (same as D16). | Decided 2026-10-02, task 4.7a. Rejected: a partial waiver of the unpaid remainder (not a reversal, so a second entry shape), and refunding the paid part (a credit balance, which D7 forbids). Pooled rather than per-fee because nothing in the ledger says which fee a payment paid. |
| D25 | **A write-off zeroes every receivable in one entry: principal is the loss, unpaid interest and fees come back out of income.** `writeOff(db, { loanId, date, reason })` posts, for what the loan owes on `date`: Dr write_offs / Cr loans_receivable (principal), Dr interest_income / Cr interest_receivable (interest), Dr fees_income / Cr fees_receivable (fees), a zero bucket's pair left out; `kind = 'write_off'`, memo `Written off <total>: <reason>`. A partial unique index `already_written_off` on `loanId` where `kind = 'write_off'` allows one per loan. Refusals, in this order: `reason_required` (blank after trimming), `unknown_loan`, then under the D16 row lock: `already_written_off`, `backdated` (the loan has an entry dated after `date`), `accrual_pending` (as D16: accrual through `date` must be posted, so the write-off takes all the interest), `not_in_default` (state on `date` is not `default_recommended`, D18). **After it the loan reads `written_off`**, with DPD frozen at its value on the write-off date; principal is zero, so accrual posts nothing and the late-fee job charges nothing (D23). Fixture WO1 (`fixtures/writeoff/`). Known ceiling: no recovery path: a payment after a write-off is refused `exceeds_payoff` (nothing is owed); a recovery would credit a `recoveries` income account, not reopen the receivables. | Decided 2026-10-02, task 4.7b. Rejected: everything to write_offs (income would keep interest and fees never collected, and the loss would include them). Standard charge-off with non-accrual reversal. |

| D27 | **Past the last due date, a loan that still owes principal is past due on its final installment.** D19 compared cash with the printed rows only, so a loan that paid the schedule's cash total but still owed (a late fee or late-payment interest taken out of an installment by the waterfall, or D4's drift when the printed last row is paid instead of the quote) read `current` for ever: no next payment due, no fee, no band, and `writeOff` refused it `not_in_default`. D9 already says the final installment due is the payoff quote, and D4 that the ledger is the truth: while loans_receivable through D is above zero the final installment is unsatisfied whatever cash covered. `dpd` then counts from the last due date once every printed row is covered; `runStatus` charges the final installment's one late fee at its grace exit (D19, D20) if principal is still owed that day; `nextDue` names the last due date and everything owed as posted.
